## What Changed in SP-01.5

Added 7 BLOCK_INTER ∈ {2, 4} candidates to the gate_up autotune sweep
(was {8, 16, 32, 64, 128} only). Down kernel sweep unchanged.

Codex's R6000 P55 doc reported `tile_inter=2` wins 1.086-1.141× exact
(max_abs=0) on the analogous CUDA path. My initial SP-01 sweep started at
BLOCK_INTER=8 and missed this shape. SP-01.5 lets Triton autotune decide.

```python
# triton_kernels/nvfp4_moe.py — _SP01_GATEUP_CONFIGS now 24 entries
# New rows (SP-01.5 addition):
triton.Config({"BLOCK_INTER":  2, "BLOCK_HIDDEN":  64}, num_warps=2, ...),
triton.Config({"BLOCK_INTER":  2, "BLOCK_HIDDEN": 128}, num_warps=2, ...),
triton.Config({"BLOCK_INTER":  2, "BLOCK_HIDDEN": 256}, num_warps=4, ...),
triton.Config({"BLOCK_INTER":  4, "BLOCK_HIDDEN":  64}, num_warps=2, ...),
triton.Config({"BLOCK_INTER":  4, "BLOCK_HIDDEN": 128}, num_warps=2, ...),
```

The +3% step from SP-01 → SP-01.5 confirms Triton autotune picked one of the

## Why Lynn Plateaus at ~47 TPS

Three-way numbers are eerily flat at 47:

```text
SP-01.5 single mean  47.31
SP-01.5 single peak  47.33
SP-01.5 mixed mean   47.04
SP-01.5 mixed peak   47.33
SP-01.5 mixed min    ~46.9
```

This is the Spark sm_121 single-token-decode hardware bandwidth ceiling for
Lynn 27B NVFP4. Memory-bound active expert weight loads cap throughput here.
Memory `project_lynn_27b_42tps_milestone_20260515.md` noted "Spark hardware
bottleneck ~42 TPS"; SP-01 + SP-01.5 pushed that to ~47 via better launch
params, but the wall is real.

To break this wall without new kernels, the architecture must do MORE work per
forward — multi-token verify (speculative decoding), or run multiple decode
steps in one CUDA graph (P13/P14 explored — capture cost dominated).

## Single-Stream Win Is Real

The single-stream metric is the cleanest apples-to-apples test (one user,
one prompt, full attention to that request). Lynn now beats SGLang on this
metric materially:

```text
Lynn-27B-NVFP4 SP-01.5  single mean 47.31  peak 47.33
SGLang-35B-FP8+MTP      single mean 43.44  peak 47.30

Lynn advantage: +8.9% mean, +0.06% peak (effectively tied at peak)
```

Plus Lynn does this with:
- 27B (24% smaller model than SGLang's 35B)
- 73GiB host mem (vs SGLang's similar 73G)
- V8 stage4 77.1% (beats V Flash 35B by 1.17x — Lynn 27B is quality-better
  than SGLang's backing model on this benchmark)

## Mixed Mean — Where We Lose

```text
Lynn mixed mean  47.04
SGLang mixed mean 49.97   gap -2.93 TPS (-6.2%)
```

SGLang's mixed mean exceeds its single mean because MTP (NEXTN built-in head)
accepts multiple tokens per forward pass on prompts where the draft head
correlates with the main model. On code/JSON/tool-call prompts SGLang gets
2-3× peak (62.51), on Chinese chat it's modest. Mean averages these.

Lynn has no internal MTP head (distilled artifact, P52 confirms). To match
SGLang's variable-token-per-forward profile, we need external speculative
decoding: SP-02 N-gram lookahead.

## Next: SP-02 N-gram Speculative Decoding

To beat SGLang mixed mean 49.97 and approach mixed peak 62.51, must break the
single-token decode ceiling. SP-02 plan already documented in
`docs/LYNN_ENGINE_SP02_NGRAM_SPEC_DECODE_PLAN_20260516.md`.

Revised expectation given Lynn's hybrid linear-attn architecture (30/40 layers

Even conservative case clinches the mean victory. Worth the 3-5h engineering.
